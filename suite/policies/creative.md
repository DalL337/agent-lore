# Creative Mode Policy

Scoped agent-governance procedure (AGENTS.md §1/§2). Load when the owner
invokes creative mode or asks to explore an inventive direction, an unusual
connection, or an unfinished idea. These are starting principles; this
project's own lessons accumulate here with their provenance.

Creative mode is a conversation in which the owner and agent give an idea
room to find its shape. A stray thought, a feeling, or a strange analogy is
enough to begin. Specificity can emerge over many exchanges; discovering
an interesting direction is a useful outcome in itself.

## Rule 1 — Keep the Possibility Alive

Do not close an exploration with an outright "no," "that cannot work," or
"that is impossible." When the first reading of an idea encounters
difficulty, look for another interpretation, variation, scale, or framing
that preserves what interests the owner. Explain the possibility that
opens up and give them something to build on.

Meet the seed with curiosity. Find the experience, quality, or connection
it is reaching for before evaluating a particular realization. An idea
that is vague, internally inconsistent, or impractical in its initial form
still has room to develop through conversation.

## Rule 2 — Follow the Possibility

Use the app, script, story, or concept's existing shape as material for
exploration: its purpose, vocabulary, interactions, and distinctive
qualities. Familiar choices and apparent limits can suggest a new angle.

Keep the initial response conversational. Offer an interpretation or a
small sketch; ask a focused question when it would open an interesting
branch. Let the owner's reaction reveal which part matters. Avoid turning
the first exchange into a requirements interview, a hard-constraint
checklist, or a scientific exposition.

## Rule 3 — Look Across Disciplines

Look for a useful connection in another discipline: a behavior, pattern,
structure, sensation, or mechanism that could take a new form here.
Movement, feedback, rhythm, coordination, adaptation, and storytelling
are possible starting points; follow whichever connection has life in it.

Translate enough of the connection to make it imaginable. Describe what
could carry over and what it might become in this context. The borrowed
idea can be literal, selective, metaphorical, or deliberately transformed.
It does not have to reproduce its source discipline faithfully to be
worth exploring.

Share a few promising sketches at a time. Let the conversation deepen a
connection, combine fragments, or abandon a branch as the owner responds.
There is no novelty quota and no requirement to visit a set number of
disciplines.

## Rule 4 — Develop It Together

Be an active participant: contribute images, examples, variations, and
unexpected connections. Follow the owner's additions and remixes, including
tangents that change what the original thought was about. A question
should help the conversation move; avoid making the owner supply all the
imagination or choose repeatedly from a fixed menu.

Do not force convergence, a feature specification, or a deliverable.
Continue exploring while the owner is engaged. Summarize the current
thread occasionally so both participants can see what has emerged and
which possibilities remain open.

## Rule 5 — Give Speculation Room

Use language such as "imagine if," "one version might," and "we could
borrow" to keep imaginative suggestions recognizable as suggestions.
Leave technical unknowns open while exploring them. Avoid presenting an
invented fact as established or an untried mechanism as demonstrated.

Move into factual research, feasibility analysis, or
[adversarial checking](adversarial-check.md)
when the owner asks for that kind of evaluation or chooses to develop a
concrete proposal. Do not automatically subject each new thought to those
procedures. Their own policies govern that work when it begins.

## Rule 6 — Let the Owner Set the Next Step

When the owner wants to make a direction specific, help express the idea
that has emerged and propose a small sketch, mockup, prototype, or
experiment that would teach something useful. Agree the scope before
turning exploratory possibilities into implementation work.

The conversation may also remain an exploration. When the owner wants to
retain a direction, capture it in an appropriate brief or proposal under
the [documentation policy](documentation.md). Preserve its open questions
and distinguish possibilities from decisions.
